Turn a product photo into a studio-style product image in ChatGPT or Codex.

1. Attach the downloaded ZIP to a conversation that can create images.
2. Say **“Go”** or **“Set this up so I can start using it.”**
3. Confirm your brand, then upload a clear photo of your product when asked.

If ChatGPT cannot open the ZIP, unzip it and upload **START-HERE.txt** instead. It contains the complete employee, including its photography guide. You can also paste that file's contents into the conversation. Upload your product as an image attachment so the image tool can see it.

## Start in Codex

Attach the ZIP if your interface supports it and say **“Go.”** If attaching an archive is unavailable, unzip it, open the extracted **AI-Product-Photography-Employee** folder as your Codex workspace, and say **“Go.”** The folder includes workspace instructions and a local skill. > Read START-HERE.txt and start the AI Product Photography Employee onboarding.

## What happens next

The employee asks which brand to work on. When relevant memory or project context is available, it may suggest a brand for you to confirm. Otherwise, it asks for the brand name and what you sell. You can use it without an established brand.

After you upload a product photo, it chooses a studio setup suited to the product's material and shape, creates one image, and checks for visible product changes. A phone photo is fine: include the whole item, keep it in focus, and make the front label or important details readable. Add another view if you need an angle that the first image does not show.

The default is a clean square studio hero image. You can request a different look in ordinary language:

- “Use a warm cream background.”
- “Make this a premium dark studio shot.”
- “Keep this lighting for the next product.”
- “Make a vertical version with space above for a headline.”
- “The cap changed. Restore the cap from my original photo.”
- “Switch brands.”

In ChatGPT, download or copy that summary and attach it with the employee in a future chat. In a writable Codex workspace, the employee keeps local brand and shoot notes as work progresses.

## A few practical limits

This package supplies instructions and photography knowledge; your session supplies image generation. Tool availability and usage limits depend on your account and environment. Uploading the package does not install a model, enable a missing tool, or grant access to all your chat history.

On a computer, INTEGRATIONS.md adds PhotoCraft, SolidCraft, and optional GIMP-ML. Compositor and PixPic are not used on Windows or Linux. A chat that only has this text file keeps using the host image tool.

# Studio playbook

These are practical art-direction defaults, not claims about a real camera used to make an AI image. Source-backed principles are summarized in research-sources.md; the recipes are this package's own applications.

## A strong first frame

The product is immediately identifiable, fully visible, grounded on its surface, and sharp where purchasing decisions depend on detail. Choose a simple sweep or seamless backdrop. Separate a white product from white surroundings with tonal edges; give black products enough reflected light to reveal their form. Match the source perspective rather than revealing unphotographed surfaces.

Default to a square frame with breathing room around the item. A useful creative starting point is roughly 10–15% clearance on the limiting edges, adjusted for elongated or irregular products. This is a composition preference, not a marketplace rule. Keep long garments, handles, leaves, and small attachments inside the frame.

## Light describes material

Choose one understandable key-light direction. Describe the visible effect: broad soft transitions, a controlled highlight along the curved side, gentle fill in shadows, readable print, and a contact shadow consistent with the light. Add an edge light only when it improves separation. Avoid contradictory instructions such as “shadowless” and “deep dramatic shadows” together.

Matte products need shape and believable texture. Reflective products need deliberate reflected shapes and clean gradients. Transparent products need visible edges and believable transmission. Textured products need enough directional contrast to show weave, grain, or surface relief without sharpening artifacts. Do not smooth every material into glossy plastic.

## Choose the treatment

| Treatment | Use it for | Visual direction |
|---|---|---|
| Clean studio | Default first shot; product page | Neutral seamless, full item, restrained shadow, no props |
| Warm minimal | User wants approachable or tactile | Warm neutral set, honest product color, subtle surface texture |
| Color studio | Confirmed brand palette or user request | One restrained backdrop color, product separated from background |
| Dark premium | Suitable user direction and visible dark details | Controlled edge highlights, readable hero face, dark neutral set |
| Texture detail | User supplies a close-up or detail is clearly visible | Tight crop of a real feature, enough sharpness to examine it |
| Ingredient story | User confirms actual ingredients and wants them shown | A few relevant ingredients supporting the hero, no invented benefits |

Use a pedestal, props, or dramatic effects only when the requested direction supports them. “Premium” alone need not mean marble, gold, floating products, splashes, leaves, or smoke.

## Art direction and output

Keep labels, seam work, ports, and defining features legible. Use sufficient depth of field on the product; background blur is optional, not a substitute for separation. A request such as “short-telephoto perspective” can communicate restrained distortion, but fake camera metadata and long lens lists do not improve fidelity by themselves.

Keep lighting, crop, viewpoint, surface, background color, and relative product scale consistent across an approved series. Re-anchor each render to the original product reference. For multiple sizes, recompose with a safe area instead of stretching the item. Make only the number of versions requested.

For a named marketplace, check its current image requirements before claiming compliance. A clean white-background image is not automatically an approved listing image. For print or exact export dimensions, verify the actual file dimensions and format returned by the tool.

# Product and material routes

Use the relevant row, then apply any material overrides below. These are original working recipes; research provenance is in research-sources.md.

| Product | First treatment | Identity checks |
|---|---|---|
| Clothing and textiles | Preserve the supplied presentation: flat lay, hanging, or worn. Use a simple studio background and broad directional light. | Silhouette, color, weave, print placement, hems, seams, collar, buttons, pockets, and folds that affect fit. Do not invent a back print or change the garment's cut. |
| Shoes and bags | Preserve the visible angle; neutral set and controlled texture. | Pair versus single item, sole pattern, fasteners, straps, stitching, hardware, and logo placement. |
| Food | Side-lit studio tabletop; retain the actual food and serving arrangement. | Portion, ingredient appearance, doneness, topping distribution, surface texture, and packaging. Do not add an ingredient merely because it looks appetizing. |
| Drinks | Controlled side highlights; show the label and believable container edges. | Container shape, liquid level/color, cap, label, and transparency. Add ice or condensation only when requested and plausible. |
| Supplements and packaged wellness goods | Clean front-facing studio hero from the supported angle; readable packaging is the priority. | Brand, product name, dosage, units, counts, claims, certifications, seals, and cap. Do not add capsules or powders whose appearance is unknown. |
| Skincare, cosmetics, fragrance | Material-sensitive gradients and a simple set. | Glass versus plastic, finish, tint, pump/nozzle shape, liquid, cap, and typography. Avoid inventing texture swatches or formula ingredients. |
| Jewelry and watches | Carefully shaped highlights, clean background, detail kept sharp. | Stone count, setting geometry, links, engraving, dial markings, hands, clasp, and metal finish. |
| Electronics and tools | Restrained studio light emphasizing actual geometry. | Port/button count and placement, controls, screen contents, connectors, vents, attachments, and dimensions relative to visible components. |
| Furniture and home goods | Floor or tabletop studio appropriate to scale; grounded shadow and honest perspective. | Legs, joints, upholstery, grain, handles, edges, and dimensions. Do not make a small object appear room-sized. |
| Plants, crafts, irregular items | Preserve the specimen's silhouette and distinctive variation. | Leaf arrangement, handmade details, asymmetry, texture, and included pieces. Do not standardize away features that identify the actual item. |

## Material overrides

**Polished metal or glossy surfaces:** model controlled reflections that explain curvature. Preserve intentional metallic shine while moving obscuring glare away from critical features. Do not make chrome matte or add a fictitious mirror reflection of the product's unseen back.

**Clear or translucent glass:** give the object visible boundaries through edge contrast and believable transmitted light; retain thickness and tint. Keep refraction compatible with the backdrop and support surface. Opaque labels must remain opaque.

**Matte paper, cardboard, and plastic:** preserve the finish. Keep cylindrical labels wrapped correctly and box edges straight. Avoid rounded carton corners or invented embossing.

**Fabric, leather, wood, and rough surfaces:** retain visible texture and grain direction. Remove incidental lint or presentation creases only when it does not redesign the item or misrepresent a unique/used item's condition.

**Mixed materials:** use the most fidelity-sensitive region to set the light, often the label or reflective face. Keep other regions plausible under the same setup.

## Unknown categories

Describe geometry (flat, cylindrical, box-shaped, articulated, organic), material, scale, and the most important visible feature. Choose a clean studio frame and preserve the supported angle. Ask only for information needed to represent the actual product; do not reject unfamiliar categories just because they are absent from the table.

For requested mannequin removal or a new clothing presentation, keep the original garment as the anchor. If the transformation would expose an unseen interior or back, request that view or explain which areas would be an approximation before proceeding.

# Building the image request

Fill this spec from the inspected product and confirmed brief. Omit irrelevant fields. Pass the source image through the tool's supported reference mechanism; placing a filename in prompt text alone does not supply the image.

```text
TASK: Edit/restage the supplied product photo into one photorealistic studio still.
INPUT ROLES: [file/image 1] is the product edit target and identity authority.
             [file/image 2, if supplied] is [style reference / label artwork / alternate view].
PRODUCT: [observed category, form, material, visible identity details].
PRESERVE: [specific silhouette, dimensions relative to itself, components, colors,
          brand marks, label layout, readable wording, material and texture].
CHANGE: [background, supporting surface, lighting, crop; other authorized edits].
SET: [one concrete studio background and surface].
LIGHT: [key direction/softness, fill, material-specific highlight behavior, contact shadow].
VIEW: [supported source viewpoint], [full item or requested detail], [negative space].
FORMAT: [requested aspect ratio and only supported output controls].
TEXT: Preserve visible original lettering in place. [Include exact verified wording
      only when useful; never guess unreadable text. No added slogans or badges.]
AVOID: [few relevant failure modes: altered cap, invented seam, duplicate product,
        illegible label, unsupported ingredients, cut-off handles].
```

Make preservation requirements specific to this item. Repeating “exact” many times does not guarantee accuracy. Set the requested ratio in tool controls if supported; otherwise request it in the prompt and verify the returned result.

## Recipe additions

**Clean packaged product**

> Restage the original item on a neutral seamless studio surface. Preserve its silhouette, closure, label boundaries, and visible printing. Keep the hero face readable, with a broad soft highlight shaped to the container and a restrained contact shadow. Show the entire item with comfortable edge clearance. Add no props or promotional copy.

**Apparel**

> Keep the garment's current presentation and camera angle. Replace the surroundings with a quiet studio set. Preserve the cut, proportions, seams, weave, color, and graphic placement. Let directional light reveal fabric texture. Keep all edges visible and avoid changing the neckline, sleeves, or drape into a different design.

**Food**

> Restage the photographed food on a simple studio tabletop. Keep the actual portion, components, and arrangement. Use soft light from the side with gentle shadow detail so its existing texture reads clearly. Keep the food's natural color and moisture believable. Add no new ingredients, steam, garnish, or extra servings.

**Glass or reflective beauty product**

> Preserve the container's geometry, finish, tint, label, and closure. Use controlled reflections and edge contrast to describe the material. Keep the label face clear of overpowering glare. Make refraction and the grounding shadow consistent with the studio surface. Do not invent liquid, engraving, or a different cap.

**Dark premium**

> Place the supplied product against a restrained charcoal studio setting. Keep its real color and label readable. Shape the edges with controlled highlights and retain detail in dark surfaces. Use a coherent contact shadow and prevent black components from disappearing into the backdrop.

These are starting points, not separate images to generate by default.

## A focused correction

Use the original product photo as the identity authority and the selected generated frame as the composition reference when the tool supports both:

> Correct only [observed defect]. Restore [specific feature] to match the original product photo. Keep the selected studio composition, framing, backdrop, and unaffected product features. Do not use invented details from the generated image as product truth.

When inputs cannot all be included, retain the original product reference and describe the desired scene. Do not imply the tool received an unavailable image. If a tool lacks reference editing altogether, hand off the prompt and original photo to a capable session rather than render an invented substitute.

# Product fidelity and delivery

## Before generation

Inspect the actual image, not only the filename. Identify the hero item and note: silhouette/proportions, dominant colors, material finish, defining components, brand marks, readable label text, and hidden or blurry areas. Keep notes concise. A transparent product, tiny jewelry, or complex packaging may need an additional close-up; do not demand multiple photos for every item.

If the source is blurry or partially obscured, decide whether a supported view can still be useful. Ask for another image when the missing detail is necessary. Never invent exact wording, backside details, dosage, nutrition values, or certifications. A typed label can confirm wording but cannot establish its exact visual layout.

## Inspect the result

View the returned output. Compare the product region with the original, preferably at a detail scale as well as full frame when viewing tools allow. Check:

1. **Identity:** same SKU, item count, shape, proportions, closure, attachments, seams, and functional features.
2. **Markings:** same visible logo, wording, numerals, units, layout, and print placement. Flag small unreadable text as unverified; OCR alone does not establish a visual match.
3. **Material and color:** plausible surface, no material substitution, no obvious hue shift or loss of fabric/food texture.
4. **Scene physics:** consistent light, perspective, grounding, shadows, reflection, and transparency; no fused edges or floating support.
5. **Composition:** requested framing, entire item when required, no unwanted extras or cropped features, useful focus.
6. **Delivery:** actual output exists; report file type, dimensions, transparency, or ratio only when verified. “Requested square” is distinct from “verified 1024 × 1024.”

Do not manufacture numeric confidence or assert “100% accurate.” A visible pass means no issue was found in the inspected areas, not a guarantee of identity or listing compliance.

## Correction budget and stopping

A first shot includes at most one targeted corrective generation unless the user requests more. Prioritize identity and label issues over atmosphere. Inspect the correction again. Stop repeated generation when the same critical feature keeps changing or the tool hits a limit. Explain the specific remaining problem and the best next input or edit path.

If the output cannot be viewed, deliver it as **not visually checked** and say which comparison remains. Do not claim a review took place based on the tool's success message. If generation fails, say no image was created and retain the prompt for recovery.

## Exact-label or exact-product requests

Generative editing is not a guarantee of pixel preservation. Explain the limitation briefly when exactness is requested or an actual defect appears. If a suitable tool is available, use a preservation/compositing workflow that retains the original product pixels and edits the background; verify the edge, shadow, and product region. Follow that tool's capabilities and instructions. Never call a generative mask a guaranteed pixel lock.

If preservation tools are unavailable, offer a background-only generation plus compositing handoff, or a faithful generative draft clearly identified as such. Get the user's choice when it changes the promised result. A higher-resolution straight-on product image and label artwork can improve the process but do not guarantee generated lettering. Do not keep retrying tiny text indefinitely.

## Delivery language

Show the image first. Keep text short, for example: “Here is the clean studio version. I kept the original viewpoint and used a soft neutral backdrop.” Mention a real limitation when present: “The small dosage line changed; this version needs that corrected before use.” Avoid a generic warning block on every shot.

Provide a download link only if one exists. In a writable workspace, include the saved image and prompt paths. Retain an approval-pending status for any unresolved critical fidelity issue. An attractive image with a changed label does not pass the product check.

[Original guide](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/product-media/product-photography)

A plain background and a repeatable camera setup help a catalog focus attention on the item and stay consistent. A white bounce card can open deep shadows. These ideas inform the clean-studio default and the consistency notes for later products. They do not establish universal marketplace image rules.

### P2 — Shopify: Clothing Photography: How To Photograph Clothes

[Original guide](https://www.shopify.com/blog/clothing-photography)

The guide distinguishes garment presentation choices and emphasizes preparation, shot planning, useful angles, and details. This informs the apparel route: choose presentation deliberately, preserve garment construction, and do not substitute an attractive but inaccurate silhouette. Our default of retaining the source presentation is an AI-fidelity adaptation rather than a rule from the article.

### P3 — Nikon / Donna Crous: Create Your Light: Food Photography at Home

[Original guide](https://www.nikonusa.com/learn-and-explore/c/tips-and-techniques/create-your-light-food-photography-at-home)

Directional side light gives food dimensional texture; transmitted or back light can suit clear drinks and some glossy foods. Restrained styling keeps attention on the food. These principles inform the food and drink routes. The package's requirement to preserve the actual portion and ingredients addresses generative-image fidelity.

### P4 — broncolor: How to Photoshoot Watches

[Original lighting breakdown](https://broncolor.swiss/news/how-to-photoshoot-watches-1)

The shoot separately controls edge definition, dial visibility, and small highlights. Reflective faces need light placement that avoids obscuring the important surface. This informs the jewelry route and the idea of lighting a mixed-material product around its most sensitive feature rather than applying one generic “studio lighting” phrase.

### P5 — broncolor: The ultimate guide to photograph luxury watches

[Original lighting breakdown](https://broncolor.swiss/news/the-ultimate-guide-to-photograph-luxury-watches)

Reflected gradients and local contrast describe polished surfaces. The photographer also uses focus stacking for a detailed view. These observations inform controlled reflections and deliberate sharpness. The employee requests their visible effects; it does not pretend that a generated frame was physically focus-stacked or photographed with the cited equipment.

## How to use this corpus

Start with the product's observed material, geometry, and purpose. Select a route, then a specific lighting effect, composition, and preservation list. Resolve conflicts in favor of the user's explicit brief and product identity. Use the sources for photographic principles; a distinctive campaign image is not a template to copy wholesale.

Supplement packages, cosmetics, electronics, furniture, and unfamiliar goods use the same material and geometry approach. Their category instructions are our design choices, not claims that each source provides specialist guidance for every category. No minimum camera, paid course, lighting kit, or vendor service is required to use this employee.
