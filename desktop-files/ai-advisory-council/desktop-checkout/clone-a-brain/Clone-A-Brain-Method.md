# The Clone-A-Brain Method

How to turn a business owner's public work, or your own, into an AI advisor that reasons like them.

This is the method behind the five advisors in the AI Advisory Council course. Each of them was researched through the same four lenses, built from real transcripts, and written into the same templates you have in this kit. You can repeat it for anyone.

A good clone does three things:

1. **It reasons like the person.** It uses their real frameworks, rules and questions, not generic business advice with their name on it.
2. **It sounds like the person.** Their tone, sentence patterns and phrases, taken from transcripts of them talking.
3. **It knows its limits.** It says where their advice does not fit you, and it never invents a story, a number or a quote.

---

## The 5 steps

1. Pick the person and their seat.
2. Gather sources.
3. Extract with the four lenses.
4. Build the brain, instructions and sources.
5. Test it and add it to your council.

---

## Step 1. Pick the person and their seat

### Pick someone worth cloning

Run each candidate through four checks:

1. **Do they have deep public material?** You want at least 10 hours of them talking (podcasts, talks, lectures) and some writing (books, essays, long posts). A person known for one viral clip is not enough.
2. **Do they explain how they think?** Some people teach their reasoning. Others only tell you what they did. You want the first kind.
3. **Is their context close enough to yours?** A venture-backed founder and a local service business owner play different games. You can still learn from both, but know which one you are getting.
4. **Do they fill a gap?** If your council already has an offers and pricing advisor, a second one adds little. Add someone for a seat you do not have: operations, finance, your industry, hiring, sales.

### Write their seat

The seat is the set of questions this advisor owns on your council. One line. Examples from the course:

- Offers, pricing, lead generation, sales and scaling
- Attention, content, social media, brand and the long game
- Product, customer experience, design, brand and founder-led leadership

The seat tells the research where to dig deepest. It also tells your council when to call on this advisor and when to leave them out.

## Step 2. Gather sources

### The source-quality ladder

Not all sources are equal. From best to weakest:

1. **Their own long-form teaching, in their own words.** Books, essays, memos, letters, lectures, workshops, their own podcast or channel. This is where the frameworks live.
2. **Long-form guest interviews (about an hour or more).** Other hosts push back, so you see how they think under pressure. This is the best source for voice and stories.
3. **Their own short posts.** Threads, newsletters, LinkedIn posts. Good for recent views and signature phrases.
4. **Reputable reporting and profiles.** For facts, numbers, dates and fair criticism.
5. **Detailed summaries by other people** (book notes, episode write-ups). Only to fill gaps. Check any framework you take from a summary against a second source.
6. **Clips, compilations, quote sites and "10 rules from X" lists.** Avoid them. Quotes on these sites are often wrong or made up. Some YouTube channels post AI-generated "interviews" that never happened.

**Transcripts beat summaries.** A summary keeps the idea and throws away the voice. A transcript keeps both.

### How many of each

| Source type | Solid first version | Great clone |
|---|---|---|
| Books or core written works | 2 to 4 (or their free companion material) | All of them |
| Essays, posts, newsletters | 5 to 10 | 15 or more |
| Long-form guest interviews (transcripts) | 5 to 6, at least 2 from the last two years | 8 to 12, at least 3 recent |
| Their own talks, lectures, channel (transcripts) | 5 to 6 | 10 to 15 |
| Articles on their track record and criticism | 10 to 15 | 25 or more |
| **Total** | **About 25 sources, 10 transcripts** | **40 or more sources, 20 or more transcripts** |

Under 10 sources, you have a sketch, not a clone.

### Cloning yourself or a mentor

The ladder is the same idea, with your own material:

1. **You talking, unscripted.** Recorded coaching calls, podcast episodes, YouTube videos, Looms, voice notes where you explain a decision.
2. **Your SOPs, playbooks, training docs and onboarding docs.** This is where your exact mechanics live.
3. **Your writing.** Posts, newsletters, emails to your team and customers.
4. **Your past answers.** Replies to common customer or team questions.
5. **A short interview.** The kit asks you 12 questions to fill the gaps nothing else covers: your mistakes, your rules, what the AI should never decide without you.

Aim for at least 3 hours of you talking, or 20,000 words of your writing, plus the interview. Most recording tools (Zoom, Google Meet, Loom, many phone voice-memo apps) can export a transcript. Use the transcript, not the audio file.

## Step 3. Extract with the four lenses

Research one lens at a time. Each lens catches things the others miss.

| Lens | What you read | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Books and writing** | Books, essays, memos, letters, newsletters, long threads | Named frameworks with exact steps, core beliefs, decision rules |
| **2. Long-form guest interviews** | Other people's podcasts and interviews | How they think when challenged, the stories they tell, their voice, how they handle pushback |
| **3. Their own channel, talks and lectures** | Their channel, podcast, keynotes, lectures, workshops | How they teach, the playbooks they repeat, diagnostic questions, voice |
| **4. Track record, recent views, limits and relationships** | Reporting, profiles, company numbers, criticism | What is actually true (numbers and dates), what they think now, where their advice does not transfer, who they agree and clash with |

### Take notes with tags

Every note gets a tag and a source. This is what makes the brain easy to build and easy to check.

```
[BELIEF] What they believe, in one or two sentences. (source)
[FRAMEWORK] A named model with its exact steps, formula or checklist. (source)
[RULE] A rule of thumb. "If ___, then ___." (source)
[DIAGNOSTIC] A question they ask first, and what the answer tells them. (source)
[PLAYBOOK:<problem>] What they tell someone with this problem. (source)
[STORY] A story they tell, what happened, the lesson they draw. (source)
[QUOTE] "Verbatim, under 25 words." (source, timestamp)
[VOICE] How they talk: phrases, sentence patterns, energy. (source)
[FACT] A number or event with its date. (source, date)
[EVOLUTION] A view that changed, with rough dates. (source)
[BLINDSPOT] Where their advice has limits or has been fairly criticized. (source)
[RELATION:<person>] A documented public interaction with another advisor. (source)
```

**Depth over breadth.** "Focus on the customer" is useless. "Before building, get 10 people to pay a deposit. If you can't, the offer is wrong, not the marketing" is useful. Capture the steps, the numbers, the thresholds and the examples.

**Reading long transcripts.** A 2-hour podcast is about 25,000 words. Read the two or three best ones in full. Search the rest for the parts that matter. Transcripts are auto-captions, so names and numbers can come out wrong. Check important numbers against a second source.

## Step 4. Build the brain, instructions and sources

Three templates, in `templates/`:

1. **`BRAIN-TEMPLATE.md`** has 13 sections: snapshot, core beliefs, signature frameworks, decision rules, how they diagnose a business, playbooks by problem, contrarian takes, voice and style, stories, blind spots, how their thinking changed, example answers, and council behavior. Fill every section from your notes. Aim for 5,000 to 12,000 words.
2. **`INSTRUCTIONS-TEMPLATE.txt`** compresses the brain into instructions **under 7,500 characters**. It has to work on its own, even if the AI never opens the brain file. Some apps cut off long instructions without warning, which is why the limit matters.
3. **`SOURCES-TEMPLATE.md`** lists every source with its link and what it added.

File names carry the person's name (`Alex-Hormozi-Brain.md`, not `Brain.md`). That way you can load several advisors into one project without files clashing.

## Step 5. Test it and add it to your council

Never trust a clone you have not tested. Run the five tests in `prompts/04-Test-Your-Clone.txt`, or say **"Test my clone"** in local mode.

1. **A question they have answered publicly.** Ask it, then compare with their real answer. Best if you pick an interview that is not in the sources, so the clone cannot just look it up.
2. **A framework test.** Ask it to walk through one of their frameworks and apply it to your business. The steps should match the source.
3. **A voice test.** It should sound like their transcripts, not like a blog post.
4. **"Are you really them?"** It must say it is an AI advisor built from their public work. Then ask about a story that never happened. It must say it has no record of it.
5. **An out-of-lane question.** Ask something far from their seat. It should say so, reason from their principles, and label that as its own extrapolation.

When a test fails, the test file tells you which part of the brain or instructions to fix.

Then add it to your council. If you have the full AI Advisory Council package, the local path does this for you. On the web, follow "add an advisor" in `council/Council-Roster.md`.

## What separates a great clone from a generic one

| Generic clone | Great clone |
|---|---|
| "Focus on value." | The exact framework, with its steps, and how to score your offer on each one. |
| Makes up a story about their early days. | Retells only stories they have told publicly, with the source. |
| Always confident, always right. | Knows its blind spots. Says when their advice does not fit your business. |
| Sounds like a blog post. | Sounds like their transcripts: sentence length, phrases, how they deliver a hard truth. |
| Quotes it cannot back up. | A quote bank of short, verbatim quotes, each with its source. |
| Frozen on views from 2019. | Separates what they think now from what they used to think. |
| Answers anything straight away. | Asks the two or three questions they would ask first. |

The five rules behind a great clone:

1. **Exact mechanics.** Steps, numbers, thresholds and examples. Not slogans.
2. **Documented stories only.** The clone may retell a story they told. It never invents a new one.
3. **Blind spots written down.** A clone that is always right is useless. Your council needs to know when to discount it.
4. **Voice from transcripts.** Read them talking. Summaries strip the voice out.
5. **No invented quotes.** A quote goes in only if you read it word for word in a source. Under 25 words. With the source.

## Keeping it current

People change their minds. A clone built once and never touched drifts.

- **When:** every 3 to 6 months, or when they release a book, launch something big or do a major interview.
- **Local:** say **"Update <name>'s brain."** It researches what is new since the last date and updates the brain.
- **Web:** run `01-Deep-Research-Prompt.txt` with lens 4 only and "only material since [MONTH YEAR]" in the MUST INCLUDE line. Then, in a new chat, attach the brain and the new research and paste:

```
Update the attached brain with the attached new research. Change only what the new research supports. Add new views to section 11 (How their thinking has changed) with dates, and mark older views that they have since changed. Update the "Researched:" date to [MONTH YEAR]. Do not remove documented stories or quotes. Show me the full updated sections only, one section at a time, and tell me which sections changed.
```

- **Then replace the file** in your ChatGPT or Claude project. Delete the old brain first, so the AI never reads two versions.

## Ethics

This is the part that keeps you and your business out of trouble.

### Public figures

- **Build only from public material:** what they published or said on the record, and what reputable outlets reported.
- **Label it an AI advisor.** "An AI advisor built from [Name]'s public work." Every template in this kit already says this.
- **Never sell it, present it as the real person, or imply they endorse you or it.** Do not post content "from" them. Do not use their name or face to market your business. Do not pair the clone with a copied voice or face of the real person.
- **Respect their work.** Paraphrase their books in your own words. Keep quotes short. Do not share transcripts or book text.
- **Be fair about blind spots.** Facts and reputable criticism, not gossip.

### Private people (a mentor, a coach, a colleague, an employee)

- **Only with their clear permission.** A message or email saying yes is enough. Keep it.
- Show them the brain before you use it. Remove anything they want removed.
- Delete it if they ask.
- Label it "an AI advisor built from [Name]'s material, with their permission. It is not [Name]."

### Cloning yourself

This is often the most useful clone. Your team gets answers in your voice, and your AI stops giving generic advice.

- **Check what you feed it.** Take out client names, contact details, passwords, account numbers and anything you would not want your whole team to read.
- **Other people's words need their consent.** Recorded calls with clients or staff: remove their details, or ask them first.
- **Decide what it must never decide without you.** Refunds, discounts, hiring and firing, legal and money commitments. The interview asks you this, and the instructions enforce it.
- **Tell your team what it is:** "an AI version of how I think. It can be wrong. Check big calls with me."
- **Sharing with your team:** ChatGPT Projects can be shared on every plan (**Share**, then invite). Claude Projects can be shared only on Team and Enterprise plans.
