# Meta ad copy playbook

The rules this copywriter follows. Last reviewed October 2026. Meta changes things often, so when the corpus or the member's own results disagree with a rule here, trust the evidence.

## Where each field shows

| Field | Where it shows | Length rule we use | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary text | Above the creative in feed. Over the video in Reels (1 to 2 lines). | The hook lands in the first 125 characters (about 70 for Reels). Total length follows the corpus. | Feed cuts to "See more" at about 125 characters. Reels shows far less. |
| Headline | Under the creative, next to the button | 40 characters max, 27 ideal | Mobile placements truncate long headlines. |
| Description | Under the headline, on some placements only | 30 characters max, 27 ideal | Often hidden, so it never carries critical information. |
| Button | Next to the headline | Pick from Meta's list | Should match what happens after the click. |

Long copy is fine when the niche uses it and the proven ads are long. Length is not the problem. A weak first line is.

## Creative diversity (why three angles, not three rewrites)

- Meta's ad delivery system matches ads to people based on what the ad actually says and shows. Ads and text that are nearly the same get treated as the same thing, so they compete for the same people instead of reaching new ones.
- Meta allows up to 5 primary texts, 5 headlines and 5 descriptions per ad as "text options". It recommends making those options meaningfully different, for example a different pain point, persona or benefit, not a few swapped words.
- So every set of three variations differs in angle, hook type, length and the reader it's for.
- Visuals add more diversity than text. When the member asks how to scale, the honest answer is new creatives (new concepts, not new crops), each with its own copy.

## What good copy looks like in 2026

- **Native, not "ad-like".** It reads like a person talking: short lines, plain words, specific details.
- **Specific beats clever.** "Raw clip in, finished short out in 4 minutes" beats "Transform your content workflow."
- **One message per ad.** The creative and the copy make the same promise.
- **Proof near the claim.** If you claim it, back it in the next line: a number, a review, or a demo.
- **A clear next step.** Say what happens after the click ("See what's inside", "Pick a time").
- **Sounds human.** No em dashes, and none of the words that read as machine-written (the checker has the list): unlock, leverage, seamless, elevate, game-changer, and so on.

## Policy red lines (Meta rejects these)

**Personal attributes.** Never assert or imply that the reader has a personal characteristic: health (physical or mental), medical conditions, financial status, age, race or ethnicity, religion or beliefs, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, criminal record, or relationship status.

| Rejected | Fine |
|---|---|
| "Are you overweight?" | "A 20-minute workout plan for busy weeks." |
| "Struggling with debt?" | "A simple way to plan your monthly budget." |
| "Other single moms are..." | "Built for parents with no spare time." |
| "Your anxiety is holding you back." | "Tools that help you feel calmer at work." |

**Income and results claims.** No promised earnings, "get rich", "quit your job", or results that aren't typical and provable. The real price of the product is fine. Earnings are not.

**Health and before/after.** No miracle cures. No unrealistic or guaranteed results, and no "lose X pounds in Y days".

**Misleading or bait.** No fake urgency (a countdown that never ends), no fake buttons drawn in the image, and no "Like and share", "Tag a friend" or "Comment below" engagement bait. No "Click here".

**Meta's own brands.** Avoid "Facebook", "Instagram" and "Meta" in copy unless there's a clear need, because Meta has brand-use rules.

**Special ad categories.** Ads about credit, employment, housing, or social issues, elections and politics run under extra rules and limited targeting. If the member is in one of these, tell them and keep the copy strictly factual.

**Style flags.** Excessive caps, repeated punctuation (!!), and lots of emojis can lower delivery or trigger review.

When unsure, write the safer version and note the risk in the copy file.

## Buttons (common ones)

Learn more, Sign up, Shop now, Subscribe, Get offer, Book now, Download, Apply now, Contact us, Get quote, Order now, Watch more, Send message. Match the button to the landing page: Sign up for a free account or community, Shop now for a store, Book now for a calendar, Learn more when the page explains before it sells.

## Hook types (to vary across variations)

| Type | Shape | Example skeleton |
|---|---|---|
| Result | Lead with the outcome | "[Outcome] in [time], without [usual pain]." |
| Demo | Describe what's happening on screen | "I [did X] and it [result]." |
| Problem | Name the situation, never the person's attribute | "Most [role]s lose [time] a week to [task]." |
| Callout | Say who it's for | "For [role]s who [situation]." |
| Contrarian | Push against a common belief | "Stop [common advice]. Do [this] instead." |
| Question | A real question with no personal attributes | "What would you do with [benefit]?" |
| Proof | Lead with a number or a quote | "[Number] [people] already [action]." |
| Offer | Lead with the deal | "[Thing] for [price]. Here's what's inside." |
| Story | Open on a moment | "[Time], I [situation]. Then [turn]." |
| Curiosity | Hold back the answer, honestly | "The [thing] nobody in [niche] talks about." |
