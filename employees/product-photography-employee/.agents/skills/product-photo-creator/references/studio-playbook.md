# Studio playbook

These are practical art-direction defaults, not claims about a real camera used to make an AI image. Source-backed principles are summarized in research-sources.md; the recipes are this package's own applications.

## A strong first frame

The product is immediately identifiable, fully visible, grounded on its surface, and sharp where purchasing decisions depend on detail. Choose a simple sweep or seamless backdrop. Separate a white product from white surroundings with tonal edges; give black products enough reflected light to reveal their form. Match the source perspective rather than revealing unphotographed surfaces.

Default to a square frame with breathing room around the item. A useful creative starting point is roughly 10–15% clearance on the limiting edges, adjusted for elongated or irregular products. This is a composition preference, not a marketplace rule. Keep long garments, handles, leaves, and small attachments inside the frame.

## Light describes material

Choose one understandable key-light direction. Describe the visible effect: broad soft transitions, a controlled highlight along the curved side, gentle fill in shadows, readable print, and a contact shadow consistent with the light. Add an edge light only when it improves separation. Avoid contradictory instructions such as “shadowless” and “deep dramatic shadows” together.

Matte products need shape and believable texture. Reflective products need deliberate reflected shapes and clean gradients. Transparent products need visible edges and believable transmission. Textured products need enough directional contrast to show weave, grain, or surface relief without sharpening artifacts. Do not smooth every material into glossy plastic.

## Choose the treatment

| Treatment | Use it for | Visual direction |
|---|---|---|
| Clean studio | Default first shot; product page | Neutral seamless, full item, restrained shadow, no props |
| Warm minimal | User wants approachable or tactile | Warm neutral set, honest product color, subtle surface texture |
| Color studio | Confirmed brand palette or user request | One restrained backdrop color, product separated from background |
| Dark premium | Suitable user direction and visible dark details | Controlled edge highlights, readable hero face, dark neutral set |
| Texture detail | User supplies a close-up or detail is clearly visible | Tight crop of a real feature, enough sharpness to examine it |
| Ingredient story | User confirms actual ingredients and wants them shown | A few relevant ingredients supporting the hero, no invented benefits |

Use a pedestal, props, or dramatic effects only when the requested direction supports them. “Premium” alone need not mean marble, gold, floating products, splashes, leaves, or smoke.

## Art direction and output

Keep labels, seam work, ports, and defining features legible. Use sufficient depth of field on the product; background blur is optional, not a substitute for separation. A request such as “short-telephoto perspective” can communicate restrained distortion, but fake camera metadata and long lens lists do not improve fidelity by themselves.

Keep lighting, crop, viewpoint, surface, background color, and relative product scale consistent across an approved series. Re-anchor each render to the original product reference. For multiple sizes, recompose with a safe area instead of stretching the item. Make only the number of versions requested.

For a named marketplace, check its current image requirements before claiming compliance. A clean white-background image is not automatically an approved listing image. For print or exact export dimensions, verify the actual file dimensions and format returned by the tool.
