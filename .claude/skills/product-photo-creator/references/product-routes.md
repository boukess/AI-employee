# Product and material routes

Use the relevant row, then apply any material overrides below. These are original working recipes; research provenance is in research-sources.md.

| Product | First treatment | Identity checks |
|---|---|---|
| Clothing and textiles | Preserve the supplied presentation: flat lay, hanging, or worn. Use a simple studio background and broad directional light. | Silhouette, color, weave, print placement, hems, seams, collar, buttons, pockets, and folds that affect fit. Do not invent a back print or change the garment's cut. |
| Shoes and bags | Preserve the visible angle; neutral set and controlled texture. | Pair versus single item, sole pattern, fasteners, straps, stitching, hardware, and logo placement. |
| Food | Side-lit studio tabletop; retain the actual food and serving arrangement. | Portion, ingredient appearance, doneness, topping distribution, surface texture, and packaging. Do not add an ingredient merely because it looks appetizing. |
| Drinks | Controlled side highlights; show the label and believable container edges. | Container shape, liquid level/color, cap, label, and transparency. Add ice or condensation only when requested and plausible. |
| Supplements and packaged wellness goods | Clean front-facing studio hero from the supported angle; readable packaging is the priority. | Brand, product name, dosage, units, counts, claims, certifications, seals, and cap. Do not add capsules or powders whose appearance is unknown. |
| Skincare, cosmetics, fragrance | Material-sensitive gradients and a simple set. | Glass versus plastic, finish, tint, pump/nozzle shape, liquid, cap, and typography. Avoid inventing texture swatches or formula ingredients. |
| Jewelry and watches | Carefully shaped highlights, clean background, detail kept sharp. | Stone count, setting geometry, links, engraving, dial markings, hands, clasp, and metal finish. |
| Electronics and tools | Restrained studio light emphasizing actual geometry. | Port/button count and placement, controls, screen contents, connectors, vents, attachments, and dimensions relative to visible components. |
| Furniture and home goods | Floor or tabletop studio appropriate to scale; grounded shadow and honest perspective. | Legs, joints, upholstery, grain, handles, edges, and dimensions. Do not make a small object appear room-sized. |
| Plants, crafts, irregular items | Preserve the specimen's silhouette and distinctive variation. | Leaf arrangement, handmade details, asymmetry, texture, and included pieces. Do not standardize away features that identify the actual item. |

## Material overrides

**Polished metal or glossy surfaces:** model controlled reflections that explain curvature. Preserve intentional metallic shine while moving obscuring glare away from critical features. Do not make chrome matte or add a fictitious mirror reflection of the product's unseen back.

**Clear or translucent glass:** give the object visible boundaries through edge contrast and believable transmitted light; retain thickness and tint. Keep refraction compatible with the backdrop and support surface. Opaque labels must remain opaque.

**Matte paper, cardboard, and plastic:** preserve the finish. Keep cylindrical labels wrapped correctly and box edges straight. Avoid rounded carton corners or invented embossing.

**Fabric, leather, wood, and rough surfaces:** retain visible texture and grain direction. Remove incidental lint or presentation creases only when it does not redesign the item or misrepresent a unique/used item's condition.

**Mixed materials:** use the most fidelity-sensitive region to set the light, often the label or reflective face. Keep other regions plausible under the same setup.

## Unknown categories

Describe geometry (flat, cylindrical, box-shaped, articulated, organic), material, scale, and the most important visible feature. Choose a clean studio frame and preserve the supported angle. Ask only for information needed to represent the actual product; do not reject unfamiliar categories just because they are absent from the table.

For requested mannequin removal or a new clothing presentation, keep the original garment as the anchor. If the transformation would expose an unseen interior or back, request that view or explain which areas would be an approximation before proceeding.
